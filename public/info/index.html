QUICK NOTE FOR YAYA

Hand wash &
washing-up liquid

Guannan's hands get irritated by washing-up liquid, but the Fresh Milk Body & Hand Wash has been fine. This is the simple shopping guide based on the labels we checked.

🧴

THE EASY VERSION

What Yaya needs to remember

For hand wash: if the label says SLS or SLES, put it back and choose a sulfate-free wash instead.

For washing dishes: washing-up liquid itself is much stronger than hand wash, so Guannan should avoid prolonged bare-hand contact and use gloves.

✋

AVOID FIRST

Ingredients to watch for

  • Sodium Laureth Sulfate (SLES)
  • Sodium Lauryl Sulfate (SLS)
  • Limonene — worth avoiding for now
  • Methylisothiazolinone (MI)
  • Methylchloroisothiazolinone (MCI)

This does not prove an allergy to any one ingredient. The biggest clue is that strong detergent products cause trouble while the milder Fresh wash does not.

✓

BETTER CHOICE

What to look for

  • Sulfate-free hand or body wash
  • A mild cleanser rather than an antibacterial/degreasing wash
  • Glycerin or other moisturising ingredients are useful
  • Milder cleansers such as sodium cocoyl isethionate, sodium cocoyl glutamate or sodium methyl cocoyl taurate

The Fresh wash uses milder cleansers and has glycerin high in the ingredient list. It has been tolerated, so there is no reason to stop using it just because it contains fragrance.

THE PRODUCTS WE CHECKED

Simple verdict

✓ KEEP USING

Fresh Milk Body & Hand Wash

No SLS or SLES on the label. It uses milder cleansing ingredients and contains glycerin. Guannan already tolerates it.

✕ SKIP FOR NOW

Blue Asda antibacterial hand wash

Sodium Laureth Sulfate (SLES) is the second ingredient. Because the aim is to avoid stronger sulfate detergents, this is not the one to try next.

✕ GLOVES / AVOID ON SKIN

Tesco antibacterial washing-up liquid

The label shows 15–30% anionic surfactants plus other surfactants and limonene. It is designed to remove grease, not to be gentle hand wash.

✕ GLOVES / AVOID ON SKIN

Dunnes lemon washing-up liquid

Contains anionic and other surfactants, limonene, and the preservatives MI/MCI. These give several possible irritation or allergy triggers.

WHY THIS MAKES SENSE

Probably irritation first, not a proven allergy

Washing-up liquid is made to strip grease. That can also strip the protective oils from skin and cause irritant contact dermatitis. This can happen even without a true allergy.

Limonene is a second suspect because it appears on both washing-up-liquid labels we checked and is not listed on the Fresh wash. That is only a clue, not proof.

Cocamidopropyl betaine, fragrance and linalool are lower on the suspect list because the Fresh wash contains them and Guannan currently tolerates it.

Worth getting checked: if the rash becomes very itchy, blistered, swollen, keeps returning despite avoiding detergents, or does not settle, a GP or dermatologist can assess it and patch testing can check for a contact allergy.
